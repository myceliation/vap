Archived October 1, 2026. Snapshot of NJ vacant & abandoned property records obtained via OPRA. Not maintained. Being on a list doesn't mean a property is currently vacant; don't trespass.

The other places on the map come from open map data and educated guesses. Most are private land, many are unsafe, and brownfields can be contaminated.

    Re:
    How these records were gathered

    Requests went to New Jersey municipal clerks in two rounds: by hand in spring 2025, mostly to South Jersey towns, and by automated email to nearly every town in May and June 2026. They asked for vacant, abandoned or foreclosed property registries, unsafe-structure orders, and long-term water shutoffs. Each town's dot shows how its most recent request was answered.

    Lists that came back as spreadsheets, PDFs or email text were typed into one table, block and lot were cleaned up, and each property was placed on its tax parcel from the statewide NJOGIS layer, with the Census geocoder as a fallback. Owner names, contacts and correspondence were left out, and so were homes the lists themselves marked as lived in.

    The other places come from OpenStreetMap features tagged abandoned, disused or ruined, from Wikidata entries marked closed or decommissioned, and from a few guesses. A tower is a guess when the FCC terminated its registration with no dismantle notice on file, or when nobody has touched its registration in twenty years. A military site is a guess because many Nike bases and coastal batteries are parks now. The census layer shades each tract by its share of homes the Census Bureau counts as vacant for reasons other than rent, sale or seasonal use, which is where long-empty houses show up.

    Treat every entry as a lead, not a fact about today.
