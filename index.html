          About this map

          NJ records

          Records requests under New Jersey's Open Public Records Act went to municipal clerks in two rounds: by hand in spring 2025, mostly to South Jersey, and by email to nearly every town in May and June 2026. They asked for vacant, abandoned or foreclosed property registries, unsafe-structure orders and long-term water shutoffs. Town dots show how the most recent request was answered.

          Lists that came back were combined into one table, block and lot were cleaned up, and each property was placed on its tax parcel from the statewide NJOGIS layer, with the Census geocoder as a fallback. Owner names, contacts and correspondence were left out, and so were homes the lists themselves marked as lived in.

          The census layer shades each tract by the share of homes vacant for reasons other than rent, sale or seasonal use. That excludes summer homes, so it's the closest census measure to long-empty houses.

          Northeast places

          • OpenStreetMap: features tagged abandoned, disused or ruined, brownfields, historic mines and mine openings, wrecks, forts and bunkers, vacant storefronts, and abandoned rail lines.
          • Wikidata: places recorded as permanently closed, decommissioned or abandoned, plus ghost towns and ruins. Demolished places are left out.
          • USGS Mineral Resources Data System: past-producing mines, plus prospects as guesses.
          • MSHA: mines listed as abandoned, or abandoned and sealed.
          • Quebec MRNF: orphaned and abandoned mine sites.
          • Nova Scotia DNRR: abandoned mine openings, with hazard ratings and a link to each report.
          • FCC Antenna Structure Registration (guesses): towers 30 m and taller whose registration was terminated with no dismantle notice, or hasn't been touched since 2006 or earlier.

          Hollow rings are guesses. Everything else is something a public source says is abandoned, closed, disused, ruined or no longer producing. Sources lag reality: some places are reused, restored or demolished.
